\chapter{Project Tracks for Student Interests}

Students should be able to bring their own interests to the program while using a common quality standard. The tracks below define feasible questions, outputs, caveats, and capstone products for different research domains. A track is a starting point, not a restriction. The same project may combine a biological question with a methods question, or a food chemistry question with a communication product.

\section{Track 1: Plant, Agriculture, and Natural Products}
\textbf{Good research questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Which tentative GC-MS hits in two plant treatments have source-backed natural product occurrence or chemical class annotations?
  \item Do cultivar, tissue, or developmental-stage groups differ in trait profiles derived from validated chemical evidence?
  \item Which compounds have pathway or occurrence context that supports follow-up targeted analysis?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bad or overbroad questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Which plant is medicinal?
  \item Did this treatment activate a pathway based only on KEGG context?
  \item Are these compounds present in the plant because LOTUS reports them in another organism?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended uafR tables and outputs:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{ChemicalOccurrences}, \texttt{ChemicalTaxonomy}, \texttt{LOTUSProfile}, and \texttt{ChemicalClasses}.
  \item \texttt{ChemicalTraits}, \texttt{ChemicalPathwayRoles}, \texttt{KEGGPathways}, and \texttt{KEGGReactions}.
  \item \texttt{SourceCoverage} and \texttt{ChemicalTraitEvidence}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended figures:} Source coverage heatmap, occurrence evidence heatmap, compound class bar chart, trait matrix heatmap, and a focused pathway-context table.

\textbf{Key caveats:} Natural product occurrence in another organism supports hypothesis generation, not direct occurrence in the student's sample. KEGG pathway membership is pathway context, not pathway activity. Tentative GC-MS annotation requires cautious wording.

\textbf{Minimum viable project:} 10 to 20 tentative compounds, one plant system or comparison, source coverage report, occurrence or class grouping dictionary, two figures, and one limitation paragraph.

\textbf{Advanced project:} Compare treatment or tissue groups using a medium-confidence trait matrix and add a targeted follow-up list for standards or MS/MS confirmation.

\textbf{Example final product:} ``Source-backed natural product and pathway-context profile of tentative volatile annotations from treated and untreated plant tissue.''

\section{Track 2: Environmental Exposure and Toxicology}
\textbf{Good research questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Which detected or queried chemicals have hazard, property, or regulatory signals that justify follow-up review?
  \item Do sample groups differ in safety-relevant trait profiles or physicochemical properties?
  \item Which chemicals combine strong source coverage with properties relevant to volatility, hydrophobicity, persistence, or exposure screening?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bad or overbroad questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Are these samples toxic?
  \item Which chemical caused the biological effect?
  \item Is a compound risky because a hazard annotation exists?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended uafR tables and outputs:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{ChemicalHazards}, \texttt{SafetyProfile}, \texttt{PubChemProperties}, and \texttt{PubChemSafety}.
  \item \texttt{ChemicalMeasurementSummary}, \texttt{ChemicalTraits}, and \texttt{DerivedGroups}.
  \item \texttt{SourceCoverage} and \texttt{ValidationSummary}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended figures:} Hazard-term summary, property scatterplot, source coverage heatmap, confidence-weighted trait matrix, and table of chemicals prioritized for follow-up.

\textbf{Key caveats:} Hazard screening is not risk assessment. Risk requires exposure, concentration, dose, route, organism, and context. Database safety annotations may come from different formulations or use cases.

\textbf{Minimum viable project:} One chemical set, a source coverage and hazard screening table, one property figure, one evidence-limited prioritization list, and a risk-assessment caveat.

\textbf{Advanced project:} Add sensitivity analysis showing how conclusions change when low-confidence safety or exposure traits are removed.

\textbf{Example final product:} ``Evidence-screening profile for tentative chemicals with safety-relevant annotations in an environmental sample set.''

\section{Track 3: Food, Flavor, Aroma, and Sensory Chemistry}
\textbf{Good research questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Which tentative volatile compounds have source-backed flavor, odor, or sensory descriptors relevant to a food or plant system?
  \item Do treatment groups differ in sensory-relevant trait profiles or physicochemical properties?
  \item Which compounds should be prioritized for standards or sensory validation?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bad or overbroad questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Which sample tastes best?
  \item Did this compound cause a sensory perception based only on a database descriptor?
  \item Are all compounds with flavor annotations relevant at the measured abundance?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended uafR tables and outputs:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{FEMAProfile}, \texttt{ChemicalTerms}, and \texttt{ChemicalTraits}.
  \item \texttt{PubChemProperties}, \texttt{ChemicalMeasurementSummary}, and \texttt{ChemicalTraitMatrix}.
  \item \texttt{ChemicalTraitEvidence}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended figures:} Sensory descriptor heatmap, abundance versus sensory evidence plot, trait count by treatment, property summary, and source coverage table for sensory evidence.

\textbf{Key caveats:} Sensory descriptors do not prove perceptibility. Concentration, odor threshold, matrix effects, mixture effects, and human or instrument validation matter.

\textbf{Minimum viable project:} 8 to 15 volatile compounds, sensory descriptor evidence map, one heatmap, one abundance or property summary, and a cautious sensory interpretation.

\textbf{Advanced project:} Add literature-derived odor thresholds where available and classify compounds as strong evidence, context evidence, or follow-up candidates.

\textbf{Example final product:} ``Source-backed sensory descriptor profile for tentative volatile annotations in a food aroma comparison.''

\section{Track 4: Biomedical, Pharmacology, and Public Health}
\textbf{Good research questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Which query chemicals have biomedical terms, pharmacologic actions, assay annotations, or target evidence in public databases?
  \item Which compounds have evidence strong enough for background discussion versus only future-work prioritization?
  \item How does bioactivity source coverage differ across a chemical set?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bad or overbroad questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Which compounds cure disease?
  \item What mechanism explains the biological effect based only on PubChem bioassay records?
  \item Are compounds clinically relevant because they have MeSH terms?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended uafR tables and outputs:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{MeSHProfile}, \texttt{PubChemBioactivity}, and \texttt{PubChemBioAssayDetails}.
  \item \texttt{ChemicalBioassays}, \texttt{ChemicalBioactivities}, \texttt{ChemicalTargets}, and \texttt{ChemicalPotencies}.
  \item \texttt{ChemicalTraitEvidence} and \texttt{LiteratureProfile}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended figures:} Bioactivity evidence summary, target evidence table, biomedical trait matrix, assay-source coverage plot, and evidence ladder figure.

\textbf{Key caveats:} Bioassay or pharmacologic annotation does not prove mechanism, efficacy, safety, clinical relevance, or activity in the student's system. Assay context, potency, target type, and evidence source matter.

\textbf{Minimum viable project:} Small chemical set, biomedical evidence map, one source coverage figure, one table of supported and unsupported claims, and a literature-context paragraph.

\textbf{Advanced project:} Compare assay-level details for a few priority compounds while keeping broad compound interpretation conservative.

\textbf{Example final product:} ``Biomedical evidence map and claim-boundary analysis for a focused set of tentative chemical annotations.''

\section{Track 5: Methods, Data Science, and Tool Evaluation}
\textbf{Good research questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item How do \texttt{chemicalTraitMatrix()} settings change grouping or similarity conclusions?
  \item Which database sources contribute the most usable evidence for a particular chemical class?
  \item How robust are project conclusions when low-confidence traits, sparse columns, or weakly covered chemicals are removed?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bad or overbroad questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What can uafR do?
  \item Which setting gives the best-looking heatmap?
  \item Can all output tables be combined into one master table without a research question?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended uafR tables and outputs:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{ValidationSummary}, \texttt{TableQuality}, \texttt{SourceDiagnostics}, and \texttt{SourceCoverage}.
  \item \texttt{ChemicalTraitMatrix}, \texttt{ChemicalTraitSimilarity}, and \texttt{ChemicalTraitReport}.
  \item \texttt{DataDictionary} and saved RDS result objects.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended figures:} Matrix sensitivity comparison, source coverage summary, sparsity plot, trait overlap heatmap, and confidence distribution.

\textbf{Key caveats:} Methods projects still need a scientific or training question. A large output inventory is not a result unless it changes how evidence is interpreted or workflows are run.

\textbf{Minimum viable project:} One chemical set, at least two matrix settings, source coverage summary, one sensitivity figure, and a recommendation memo.

\textbf{Advanced project:} Build a reusable project-specific audit function or visualization function and test it against a second chemical set.

\textbf{Example final product:} ``Sensitivity of source-backed chemical grouping to trait matrix profile and confidence thresholds in uafR.''

\section{Track 6: Chemistry Education and Communication}
\textbf{Good research questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Which chemical traits are most useful for teaching new students about evidence limits?
  \item Can a small compound set teach identity ambiguity, source coverage, and cautious interpretation?
  \item Which visualization best communicates tentative annotation and database evidence to non-specialists?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bad or overbroad questions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item How can chemistry be made easy?
  \item Can uncertainty be removed from the training materials?
  \item Which graphic looks most engaging without preserving source evidence?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended uafR tables and outputs:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{ChemicalTraitReport}, \texttt{ChemicalTraitEvidence}, and \texttt{DataDictionary}.
  \item \texttt{SourceCoverage}, \texttt{ValidationSummary}, \texttt{ChemicalTerms}, and selected identity tables.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended products:} Teaching dataset, annotated figure set, evidence interpretation guide, worksheet example packet, and short mini-workshop.

\textbf{Key caveats:} Communication products must preserve scientific caution. Simplification should not erase tentative identity, missingness, source limitations, or the distinction between source annotation and sample evidence.

\textbf{Minimum viable project:} One teaching compound set, evidence map, two annotated figures, a short guide, and an assessment question set.

\textbf{Advanced project:} Pilot the material with a peer group and revise based on documented confusion points.

\textbf{Example final product:} ``A teaching module for interpreting tentative GC-MS annotations and public database evidence with uafR.''

\section{Capstone Options}
Students can finish with one of several products:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Manuscript-style research report suitable for internal dsDNA Core review.
  \item Poster-ready figure set with captions and methods.
  \item Reusable analysis pipeline for a larger lab or collaborator project.
  \item Database evidence atlas for a chemical class.
  \item Methods comparison or sensitivity analysis for uafR workflows.
  \item Teaching module for future interns.
\end{itemize}
